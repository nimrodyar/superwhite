What happens to a profile photo on upload

LinkedIn runs at least two different image pipelines. Feed images and company page logos are decoded, resized and re-encoded as JPEG with the colour profile carried across. That is why glowing company logos and glowing post images are a reliable effect.

The personal profile photo goes through a separate avatar pipeline built for cropping, circular masking and serving at many sizes. Independent testers who fetched the file LinkedIn serves back after upload report that the avatar path converts the image to PNG and strips every colour chunk, including the ICC profile. Without the profile the PQ-encoded pixels are read as ordinary sRGB and the photo renders as a flat, slightly muted version of itself. Superwhite's own FAQ has said the same thing since the tool launched: the profile photo is the one surface where reports conflict, and where you should not rely on the glow.

If you saw a glowing avatar on LinkedIn, check whether it belonged to a company page rather than a person. Company logos render in the same circular tile in the feed and comments, and those do glow. Most sightings of a "glowing profile picture" are company page logos.

Test it yourself in two minutes

  1. Export your photo as an HDR JPEG. In Superwhite drop the image in, set the glow threshold so only the highlights you want are affected, and export at 400px.
  2. Upload it as your profile photo without cropping in LinkedIn's editor. Any crop or filter step re-renders the image and drops the profile before the pipeline even starts.
  3. Open your own profile on an HDR phone or a recent MacBook. If the highlights glow above the white page, your account is on a pipeline that preserved the profile. If it looks normal, the profile was stripped and the photo is an ordinary photo, which is a safe outcome: nothing looks broken.
  4. If you want a definitive answer, right-click the served avatar in a desktop browser, open it in a new tab, and check the file type. A PNG means the profile is gone.

Whatever the result, the file is harmless. An HDR export that lands on a pipeline without profile support just looks like the original photo.

Glow in your banner

The profile banner is served through the general image pipeline in most tests, and a banner with a single bright accent is a strong way to make a personal profile stand out on an HDR screen. Export at 1584 × 396 with the Creator tier and upload it directly. As with any surface, check it on an HDR phone after upload.

Photo tips for HDR export

  • Decide what should glow. A photo has many near-white pixels: skin highlights, a white shirt, a bright sky. Set the glow threshold high so only the brightest one or two elements cross it. A face that glows all over looks like an overexposure.
  • Rim light and catchlights are ideal. A small bright edge on the hair or shoulder reads as a professional lighting choice on SDR and as a striking accent on HDR.
  • Keep the background dark or saturated. A white studio background will glow as a block and swallow the subject. Colour or dark grey lets the highlight do the work.
  • Do not re-save. The export is final. Any editor, compressor or messaging app between Superwhite and LinkedIn rewrites the file.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make my LinkedIn profile picture glow?

Not reliably. LinkedIn's avatar pipeline is separate from the feed image pipeline and independent tests report it converts the upload to PNG and strips the colour profile that carries the HDR signal. Company page logos, image posts and banners do keep the profile and are the surfaces where the glow works.

Why did my HDR profile photo upload look normal?

The avatar pipeline removed the ICC profile, so the PQ-encoded pixels were read as ordinary sRGB. The photo is not damaged; it simply renders as a standard image. Use the same file as a post image or company logo and it will glow.

Does a glowing LinkedIn banner work?

In most tests the banner goes through the general image pipeline, which preserves the colour profile. Export at 1584 by 396 pixels, upload it directly without cropping, and check it from an HDR phone after upload.

Which LinkedIn surfaces reliably show the HDR glow?

Image posts in the feed, company page logos, and Sponsored Content image ads. Video, carousel documents and personal profile photos do not.

Does the glow harm the photo on screens that cannot show HDR?

No. On SDR screens and on any pipeline that strips the profile, the image looks like the original photo. The effect is additive on screens that support it and invisible everywhere else.

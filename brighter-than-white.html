Can an image be brighter than white?

By Nimrod Yarkoni · Updated 17 November 2026

Yes. On a compatible HDR display, an HDR image can use brightness above the ordinary white of a web page. It is extra light from the screen, not a painted halo. On a screen or browser without the required support, that extra brightness will not appear.

White is not always the screen's maximum brightness

The white of a document, web page or app is usually part of the standard dynamic range interface. An HDR-capable display can have extra brightness available for supported images or video. This difference is often called HDR headroom, or extended dynamic range on Apple devices.

How much headroom is available changes with the screen, brightness setting, power mode and what else is displayed. There is no single brightness value for every page's white, and a format's encoded target is not a promise of measured output from your panel.

A filter may make the surrounding picture darker so white appears more striking. That is a useful design choice, but it is different from an HDR image using additional display brightness.

Why do some images look unusually bright on my phone?

They may contain HDR information that your phone and app can display. That does not mean every bright-looking image is HDR: local contrast, surrounding colours and screen settings also affect perception. Compare it with ordinary white on the same display, and use a known test rather than judging from a screenshot.

When both patches look the same

The display may be SDR, HDR may be off, power saving may reduce headroom, or the browser may not support this CSS demonstration. Run the free HDR display test and test the exported image separately. A CSS patch and an HDR JPEG use different rendering paths.

Frequently asked questions

Can white be brighter than white?

An HDR-capable screen can render supported content brighter than ordinary interface white when it has available headroom. The exact difference depends on the screen and settings.

Can filter: brightness() make an ordinary image HDR?

No. It changes the image appearance but does not supply the missing HDR encoding and colour information.

Will everyone see the same effect?

No. The display, operating system, app, browser and image delivery path affect support and brightness. Check an SDR fallback as well as HDR.

Can I share a screenshot of the glow?

A normal SDR screenshot cannot reproduce the extra display light. Share a link to a live example or the unchanged HDR file instead.

Looking for the LinkedIn glow prompt?

PUBLISHED 3 AUGUST 2026 · BY NIMROD YARKONI

Short answer: there is no LinkedIn glow prompt, because the glow is not something an AI can draw. The glowing images in the LinkedIn feed are ordinary JPEGs carrying a Rec.2100 PQ HDR color profile. The glow lives in the file's encoding, not in its pixels, so no prompt to ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Midjourney can output a file that actually glows. The working recipe is one step: run your image through Superwhite and post the exported file to LinkedIn unedited.

Why prompts can't produce the glow

When you ask an AI image tool for a "glowing logo", it paints light-colored pixels and halos. That renders at normal brightness on every screen, like any other image. The LinkedIn glow works differently: the file tells HDR displays to render its whites in the panel's reserve brightness, above the level the interface uses for white. That instruction is an ICC color profile inside the JPEG. Image generators and chat assistants output standard SDR files without it, so their "glow" is just a picture of glow.

The actual recipe

  1. Make or pick your image anywhere you like, including with AI. A white mark on a colored background works best.
  2. Drop it into superwhite.app, set the peak brightness and glow threshold.
  3. Download the JPEG and post the file directly to LinkedIn. Don't crop or edit it in the composer, that strips the profile.

That's the whole recipe. On HDR screens (recent iPhones, Pixels, MacBooks) your image out-glows the feed. On other screens it looks normal.

How it works

Your image gets re-encoded with a Rec.2100 PQ color profile, the same signal HDR video uses. On HDR screens the bright parts render with the display's spare brightness, above normal white. Everything happens locally in your browser.

Frequently asked questions

What is Superwhite HDR?

Superwhite is an HDR image tool at superwhite.app. It embeds a Rec.2100 PQ HDR profile into a JPEG so the whites render brighter than your screen's normal white on HDR displays. It is built for LinkedIn, where the effect survives image compression, and everything runs locally in your browser. It is not related to the older open source superwhite video demo on GitHub, which shows a bright white color using a small HDR video file.

How can I make my image glow brighter than white?

Superwhite embeds a Rec.2100 PQ HDR color profile into your JPEG. On a screen with HDR turned on, the bright parts of the image use the display's extra brightness headroom, so they render brighter than normal white. It runs in your browser and nothing gets uploaded.

What is the HDR trick people are using on LinkedIn?

Some LinkedIn posts show images with whites that look like they're glowing on phones and HDR monitors. That comes from an HDR color profile embedded in the JPEG, a trick that survives LinkedIn's compression because LinkedIn strips other HDR metadata but keeps the color profile. Superwhite does that embedding for you in one click.

How did Wiz get their logo to glow on LinkedIn?

The glowing Wiz logo in the LinkedIn feed is a regular JPEG with an HDR color profile embedded, made with Superwhite. On HDR screens the white mark renders brighter than the page around it, so it looks lit up while everything else stays normal. High-contrast logos work best: a white mark on a colored background. Why does the image look brighter on my phone than on my laptop?

The glow only shows up on HDR-capable displays with HDR turned on and battery saver off. Most recent phones qualify by default; plenty of laptops and older monitors don't, so the same file just looks like a normal photo there, slightly dimmer whites, nothing broken.

Does this work on Instagram or other platforms?

Not reliably yet. LinkedIn keeps the ICC color profile through its compression pipeline, which is what makes this trick work there. Instagram and most other platforms strip it or need a different HDR format (gain maps), which Superwhite doesn't support today.

Will the effect survive if I screenshot or repost the image?

No. Screenshots, re-saving in an editor, and apps like WhatsApp or iMessage strip the color profile, which kills the effect. Post the original file Superwhite gives you, and don't crop or edit it in LinkedIn's composer.

How do I make an image glow online?

There are two very different things called a glow. Most online glow tools paint a halo effect into the pixels, which shows on every screen and survives screenshots but just looks like editing. Superwhite does the other kind: it re-encodes your image with an HDR color profile so the bright parts emit real extra light on HDR screens. No painted halo, no filter, and it runs in your browser.

Is there an AI prompt or recipe for the glowing LinkedIn image effect?

No prompt can produce it, because the glow is not in the pixels. It comes from a Rec.2100 PQ color profile embedded in the JPEG file itself, which image generators and editors don't output. The recipe is one step: drop your image in above, set the brightness, and post the exported file to LinkedIn unedited.

Does HDR video work on LinkedIn like the glowing images?

Not the same way. Video pipelines on major platforms tone-map or clamp HDR brightness after creators abused it in short-form feeds. The image route is different: LinkedIn keeps the ICC color profile in JPEGs through compression, which is exactly what Superwhite uses, so the glow survives in feed images today.

How is Superwhite different from tools that just tag an image as HDR?

Tagging an image with an HDR profile without touching the pixels boosts everything at once, including JPEG compression noise, which shows up as blotchy artifacts at HDR brightness. Superwhite remaps the pixels onto the PQ curve with a glow threshold you control, flattens near-white noise, and encodes with dithering and full-quality chroma, so the mark glows cleanly while the rest of the image stays put.

How to use an HDR image in email

By Nimrod Yarkoni · Updated 17 November 2026

An HDR email image can make a small white logo or highlight brighter than ordinary white on a compatible display. Start by testing Apple Mail or iOS Mail with the original JPEG. Email clients and sending platforms handle images differently, so a working browser preview is only the first check.

Choose an accent, not the whole message

Keep the subject, offer, body copy and call to action in normal email text. Use HDR for a modest decorative accent that adds something on a supporting screen. The recipient must still understand the message when images are blocked, the client shows SDR, or the glow disappears.

Make the header or logo

  1. Prepare the artwork with its intended background. Superwhite exports JPEG, so it cannot retain a transparent background.
  2. Load it into Superwhite. Preview a restrained glow first.
  3. Choose Simple for exports up to 400px, or Creator for artwork up to 1400px.
  4. Save the HDR JPEG and keep the original SDR artwork for fallback testing.

A 400px image can be enough for a small signature mark. A wider email header may need Creator. Choose the size for the actual layout rather than buying a larger tier just because the image is for work.

Send a real test through your email platform

Your email builder may resize or recompress images when you upload them. An image proxy may fetch and transform them later. A screenshot in a campaign preview cannot establish whether the original profile survived.

  1. Send to your own test inboxes using the actual campaign or transactional email workflow.
  2. Open the message in Apple Mail and iOS Mail on an HDR-capable device, then compare with the original file.
  3. Check Gmail, Outlook and any clients important to your audience for readability and acceptable colours. Do not assume they preserve this HDR effect.
  4. Repeat with images blocked, in dark mode and with power saving enabled.

For a signature, test replies and forwards as well. Those paths can rewrap or replace images. A result in the first received message does not guarantee the same result after forwarding.

Build a fallback that earns its place

Remote images can be blocked and HDR support varies. Keep logos recognisable without extra brightness, place important information outside images, and include a web-view link to a page you control. If your sending setup can reliably choose a separate SDR rendition, test that path too. Do not depend on one media query working across every inbox.

Do not promise universal glow. This is a format-specific enhancement for tested clients. It is not a fix for dark-mode image inversion or a guarantee of higher open or click rates.

What to do if the glow disappears

Open the exported file directly on the same device. If it works there, inspect your sender's image processing and the received image URL. If it does not, start with the display test. For a more controllable first experiment, follow the website image guide.

Frequently asked questions

Which email clients should I test first?

Start with Apple Mail and iOS Mail on HDR-capable devices, using the original JPEG. Test your actual sending platform and recipient devices; support is not guaranteed across all clients.

Will an HDR email image glow in Gmail or Outlook?

Do not rely on it. Image proxies, client versions, devices and sending platforms can change the result. Test readability and SDR behaviour for those recipients.

Is this a solution for email dark mode?

No. HDR brightness and dark-mode colour inversion are different behaviours. Test both and keep essential information in ordinary email text.

Do I need Creator for an email signature?

Not necessarily. Simple exports up to 400px, which can suit a small logo. Creator exports up to 1400px for larger headers or artwork.
